> **Scope:** Mandatory engineering procedure for AI-assisted work on ArchLucid and SecureNow, including ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor and delegated agents. Internal contributor guidance; not product assurance or a claim of universal automated enforcement.

# Common AI-assisted development governance

**Owner directive:** effective immediately (2026-11-26). Apply this procedure to engineering work initiated by or performed for the owner, regardless of agent, model or client. This document coordinates existing requirements; it does not create competing architectural standards or authorize new operational capabilities.

## 1. Load the canonical instructions

Before substantive repository work, identify the repository, target branch/revision and requested scope. Read the current root [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), [engineering agent guidance](AGENTS.md), applicable scoped instructions (including [UI AGENTS.md](../../archlucid-ui/AGENTS.md) where relevant), [architecture invariant catalog](../library/ARCHITECTURE_INVARIANTS.md), and applicable accepted ADRs. For Cursor, also follow the active architecture-invariants and shared-component impact-analysis MDC rules when present. Do not treat an archived or proposed rule as an active repository rule. If a required rule is unavailable, disclose that limitation and follow the substantive requirements in this document and the available canonical sources; do not silently assume compliance.

ChatGPT conversations using a repository connector must explicitly retrieve the relevant instructions rather than assume that Cursor MDC rules or nested AGENTS.md files have been injected automatically. Codex and Cursor must respect their scoped instruction-loading mechanisms. Refresh relevant instructions when the target revision or task scope changes. Repository guidance is subordinate to platform safety constraints, the user's current authorization and accepted architectural decisions. Surface conflicts rather than bypass them.

## 2. Mandatory engineering workflow

1. **Diagnose before editing.** Identify the actual defect or requested outcome, acceptance criteria and relevant evidence. Reproduce defective behavior with a focused test when feasible; distinguish a hypothesis from a verified defect.
2. **Identify invariants and architectural reuse.** Read relevant invariant IDs and accepted decisions; locate existing extension points, interfaces, implementations and tests before creating new infrastructure. Preserve tenant and product isolation, authorization, audit/evidence integrity, cost controls, production safety, and architectural boundaries. No silent weakening of an invariant, accepted decision or enforcement mechanism.
3. **Perform shared-component impact analysis.** For common implementations, cross-layer contracts, common configuration and infrastructure, trace direct and transitive consumers to the affected behavior boundary. Inspect DI, interfaces, dynamic consumers, generated clients, persistence, deployment and test dependencies where relevant. Record known impacts, searched scope, unknowns, preserved behavioral/security contracts and a risk-adjusted verification plan before implementation. If the dependency boundary cannot be established, investigate, narrow the change or disclose the blocker; unknown impact is not zero impact. Reuse existing investigation evidence and avoid needless paperwork.
4. **Implement the smallest sound change.** Stay within authorized files, behavior and branch scope. Reuse canonical implementations rather than duplicate services, schemas or enforcement mechanisms. Maintain API, serialization, data compatibility, error semantics, concurrency and legitimate consumer behavior unless a contract change is explicitly authorized and governed. Protect existing user edits and isolate unrelated changes.
5. **Verify affected behavior.** Select tests by risk, contracts and consumer impact. Preserve test assertions, analyzers, test discovery, filters and CI gates. Never remove, weaken, skip or suppress them merely to produce green results. Apply [the test-integrity review procedure](#23-mandatory-test-integrity-and-regression-preservation) before changing what a test proves or whether it runs. Add regression and negative-control coverage as appropriate; use independent deterministic evidence for consequential claims. Independently review material shared/security changes when a genuinely separate reviewer is available and authorized. Otherwise perform a clearly labeled self-review and disclose missing independent review; do not pretend a second agent was used.
6. **Report actual evidence and unresolved work.** Reconcile the final diff with the impact assessment, applicable invariants and requested scope. Distinguish implemented, verified, failed, not run, blocked and deferred. Record source revision, changed paths, commands, observed results, test counts when available, remaining limitations, and PR status. A timeout, zero discovered tests, skipped check, mock-only result or absence of errors does not establish full behavioral correctness.

## 2.1. Mandatory parallel-agent architectural coordination

**Centralize architectural coordination; decentralize implementation.** Applies to Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex and any authorized parallel or sequential agents whose work overlaps. It does not authorize spawning agents, starting background work, changing branches or merging without applicable permission. The coordinating agent owns integration; independent work need not wait on unrelated tasks.

### Before dispatch or implementation

- Establish the current target revision, requested outcome, relevant invariant IDs and likely shared components. Search the actual repository, canonical project map, existing extension points, active backlog, relevant open PRs and known in-flight assignments for overlapping capabilities and contracts. Search by responsibility and behavior, not just proposed type or file name. Treat unavailable agent-local workspaces or incomplete searches as unknown coordination coverage, not proof that nothing overlaps.
- Reuse or extend the existing component when its responsibility and contract fit. If a new shared capability is justified, record a concise rationale, proposed contract and owning implementation task before parallel consumers begin depending on it. Do not create another service, abstraction, schema, registry or policy evaluator merely because another implementation is not yet merged.
- Maintain a lightweight coordination record in existing task/issue/PR descriptions or a shared parent plan accessible to the participating agents. Record capability or responsibility, existing canonical implementation or proposed seam, implementation owner, branch/PR, dependencies, affected paths/contracts, acceptance criteria and status. For one bounded change, a brief note in its PR is sufficient. Do not introduce a second permanent component catalog or a new product-runtime coordination service just for this process.
- Assign a single implementation owner for each overlapping shared capability or contract; identify dependent integration work and agree on the interface, semantics, version and test expectations. Independent agents may implement disjoint consumers against an agreed contract using appropriate fixtures; distinguish fixture verification from actual integration verification. Avoid concurrent writes to the same files or conflicting contracts. Use isolated branches/worktrees when supported, preserve working-tree safety, and follow current agent execution policy.
- If a potential overlap cannot be resolved from available evidence, coordinate with the owner or parent agent, narrow the work or proceed with independent nonoverlapping tasks. Do not silently invent a competing implementation or claim an exclusive reservation that other agents cannot observe. Record stale or inaccessible coordination records and unresolved dependencies.

### During parallel work

- Keep the coordination record current when scope, contracts, dependencies or ownership change; promptly surface overlap to the coordinator. Do not unilaterally change a shared contract after consumer work has started. Reassign ownership explicitly if needed.
- Recheck current upstream source and relevant open PRs before introducing or modifying reusable functionality and before integration. A passing local test or a conflict-free textual merge is not evidence that competing abstractions are architecturally compatible.
- Preserve mandatory shared-component impact analysis, architectural invariants, authorization/tenant boundaries, test integrity, resource budgets and release-branch restrictions. Do not wait unnecessarily for unrelated work; serialize only conflicting architectural decisions, shared contract edits and dependent integration steps.

### Integration and completion

- The coordinator reviews the combined intended changes against the current integration baseline, not merely each individual PR. Check semantic duplication, competing ownership, dependency direction, contract/version compatibility, DI registration, shared state, persistence/API compatibility and applicable invariants. Reconcile overlap into the canonical component where appropriate; do not merge competing implementations simply because each branch is green.
- Integrate prerequisite contracts/components before dependent consumers, or verify the coherent combined change on an authorized integration branch. Run risk-adjusted affected-consumer and cross-component regression checks against the actual integrated revision; reuse existing evidence only when its revision and scope remain applicable. No unnecessary comprehensive builds or gate bypasses.
- Report the coordinator/owners, affected shared seams, integration order, combined-diff review evidence, checks performed, outstanding conflicts or unverified dependencies, and actual PR/merge status. If integration cannot be verified, label it incomplete; do not claim that all agents or worktrees were inspected when they were not.

This is a mandatory coordination procedure, not an atomic locking service or automatically enforced global agent registry. Existing Git/PR, review, architecture test and CI mechanisms remain authoritative. Separate authorization is required to implement new automation or enforcement.

## 2.2. Mandatory root-cause and systemic architectural remediation

**Correct the cause, not merely the symptom; make the smallest sound durable correction within authorized scope.** This standing rule applies to defect investigation and remediation across ArchLucid and SecureNow, including Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex and delegated agents, in production code, tests, configuration, infrastructure and delivery tooling. It concerns engineering defects in these products, not authority to remediate a customer's environment.

### Diagnose and establish the causal boundary

- Before implementation, identify the intended behavior, observed symptom, relevant requirement or invariant, reproducible evidence where feasible, and the mechanism that permits the defect. Distinguish a confirmed cause, candidate cause and unresolved diagnosis; do not equate correlation, repeated symptoms or model agreement with proof.
- Investigate whether the defect is isolated or represents a recurring architectural deficiency. Search by failure mechanism, responsibility and contract for sibling implementations, direct/transitive consumers, shared configuration, common abstractions, pending changes and known defects. Inspect applicable enforcement and independently sourced expected behavior. Record searched scope, known affected components and unknowns proportionally; do not claim repository-wide coverage from a narrow search.
- Identify the highest verified causal layer at which a correction is safe and justified. Locate and reuse the existing canonical implementation, shared boundary or prevention mechanism. Check the current [architecture invariant catalog](../library/ARCHITECTURE_INVARIANTS.md) and applicable accepted ADRs; identify missing or ineffective enforcement. Propose a new or revised invariant only when a durable cross-cutting requirement is justified, through existing governance. Do not invent a new architectural invariant or change an accepted decision without authorization.

### Select and authorize the correction

| Situation | Required disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Isolated defect | Correct the proven mechanism locally and preserve intended behavior with focused regression evidence. |
| Confirmed shared or recurring defect | Prefer a durable correction in the canonical shared implementation or enforcement boundary; check affected consumers and related instances. |
| Broad architectural deficiency | Establish affected scope, alternatives, dependencies, compatibility, migration/rollback needs, risks, acceptance criteria and staged verification; obtain appropriate authorization before materially expanding implementation. |
| Urgent stabilization or release blocker | Apply a safe, narrowly scoped containment only if warranted; preserve invariants and required gates, identify residual risk and record separately tracked systemic remediation before declaring the work complete. |
| Uncertain root cause or insufficient authority | Continue bounded evidence gathering, narrow to an independently justified correction or report the blocker; do not launch speculative redesign or present an unverified hypothesis as established. |

- Compare tactical correction, shared correction and structural prevention by causal evidence, recurrence risk, affected scope, disruption, compatibility and cost. A larger diff is not inherently a better root-cause fix; a smaller diff is not inherently adequate. Do not introduce repetitive local patches when a proven, authorized common correction is feasible.
- Preserve the shared-component impact analysis and parallel-agent coordination procedures. Obtain explicit authorization for material scope expansion, incompatible contract changes or changes to accepted architecture. Coordinate shared ownership and dependent changes; avoid unrelated cleanup or speculative refactoring.
- A temporary mitigation must not knowingly violate a mandatory invariant, weaken authorization, suppress tests or bypass required release gates. If safe containment is not possible, escalate the blocker rather than obscure it. A release deadline does not itself authorize an exception.

### Durable prevention, tracking and closure

- Reuse the [automatic regression policy](../assurance/AUTOMATIC_REGRESSION_POLICY.md), existing defect-prevention controls and [test execution model](../library/TEST_EXECUTION_MODEL.md). Verify the causal correction with independently justified regression/negative controls, preferably red before and green after; check affected sibling cases and direct/transitive consumers. Prefer structural prevention or focused analyzer/architecture-test enforcement when it can eliminate the defect class; do not introduce a duplicate guard or claim prevention without evidence.
- If systemic remediation is deferred, blocked or only partially delivered, locate and update the existing [active technical backlog](../ACTIVE_TECHNICAL_BACKLOG.md) item or propose a deduplicated linked entry using the existing process. Record evidence and affected scope, residual risk, priority, proposed owner, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and a review trigger or target milestone when agreed. Link the tactical PR and systemic follow-up. Do not silently drop unresolved work or create competing entries. If backlog write access or authorization is unavailable, report the concrete proposed entry and blocker.
- Report local symptom resolution separately from verified systemic cause correction and recurrence prevention. Include causal evidence, applicable invariant IDs, searched/affected scope, correction choice, tests actually run, unresolved risks and actual backlog/PR status. Do not close a systemic defect merely because the initial regression passes or a temporary mitigation was merged.
- Keep verification risk-adjusted and within authorized resource and release-branch limits. A broad investigation does not authorize comprehensive master CI, automatic merges, production deployment or customer mutation. This is mandatory procedural agent guidance, not a claim of universal executable enforcement.

## 2.3. Mandatory test integrity and regression preservation

This procedure applies to ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, and delegated agents. Preserve the intended contract and the ability to detect regressions. A green result, a generated snapshot, or the implementation's current output does not establish the intended behavior.

### Identify material changes before editing

A test change is material if it changes what behavior passes or fails, what is exercised, or how a result is counted. Review changed or removed assertions, expected values, snapshots and golden files, broadened tolerances, mocks or fixture substitutions, negative controls, skips or quarantines, filters, discovery, exclusions, and any test or CI settings that remove or narrow verification. A move, rename, or helper extraction is not material only if the effective assertions, exercised dependencies, and discovery are preserved. Verify that equivalence with the affected tests.

Identify the intended contract and the test's original purpose before proposing a material change. Preserve the original assertion or an equivalent negative control while diagnosing the mismatch. Do not preserve an expectation independently shown to be wrong, or treat a newly failing test as authority to change the contract.

### Classify the mismatch

| Finding | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| Product defect | Keep the intended assertion and correct the product at the proven cause. Add or retain a regression that would detect the defect. |
| Erroneous test | Cite the contract independently of the implementation. Correct the wrong expectation or fixture and use a negative control to show that behavior outside the intended contract still fails. Do not classify the erroneous expectation as a product defect. |
| Authorized contract evolution | Cite the accepted requirement, ADR, or explicit owner decision. Update only the affected expectations, explain before/after behavior, and verify affected consumers and compatibility. Without authorization, leave the changed expectation as a proposal. |
| Environment failure or flake | Diagnose the setup, dependency, timing, isolation, or runner problem without weakening the oracle. Report the first failure and any retry separately. A proposed temporary quarantine needs an explicit owner-approved scope, rationale, revisit condition, and disclosed coverage loss or replacement verification. It does not turn the original run into a pass. |

If the cause is not known, report it as unresolved. Keep testing the competing explanations instead of choosing the one that makes the suite green.

### Record evidence and obtain review

- Before accepting a material edit, record the source of intended behavior, what the old and proposed tests prove, affected consumers, and any coverage lost or replaced. Cite the relevant requirement, ADR, invariant, or independently justified expected outcome. Actual behavior or regenerated output alone is not a source of intent.
- In the PR or existing review record, show the evidence, the proposed disposition, and any test-scope changes. Before integrating a material change, obtain explicit review of the intended behavior and coverage impact and record the reviewer and disposition. If the required review is unavailable, disclose that and leave it pending. Self-review is not approval.
- For a reproducible defect, execute the **same regression** against the defective and corrected states. It must fail for the expected defect-specific reason before correction and pass afterward. If the new test is not present in the baseline, carry the same test into an isolated baseline fixture. Reuse the [automatic regression policy and FND-166 evidence](../assurance/AUTOMATIC_REGRESSION_POLICY.md#critical-defect-intake-and-ci) for confirmed defects in its scope.
- When a faithful replay is infeasible, explain why and supply an independently justified structural check, reference comparison, or property test. State what that alternative detects and what it does not prove. A missing or inconclusive alternative remains unverified.
- Record actual revisions, commands or test identities, executed counts, observed outcomes, and any available evidence artifact. Distinguish missing, skipped, timed out, zero-test, infrastructure-only, and unrelated failures from verified coverage. Do not claim verification for anything that did not run or did not demonstrate the stated behavior.

### Test-integrity review examples

These examples are review exercises, not executable PR policy.

| Case | Proposed change or observation | Review outcome | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TI-01 | Move an exact equality assertion into a helper, retain its negative control, and check that the same tests are discovered and pass. | Accept scoped refactor | Effective behavior and coverage are unchanged. |
| TI-02 | A test expects a null collection, but an accepted contract requires an empty collection. Correct the expectation and show null still fails. | Review erroneous test correction | The contract, not today's implementation, supplies the expected result. |
| TI-03 | An approved versioned contract changes ordering. Update the ordering assertion and run the affected consumer cases. | Review authorized contract change | Explicit approval and compatibility evidence support the narrow change. |
| TI-04 | A sorting regression puts equal keys in the wrong order. Keep the required order and show the same test fails for that discrepancy before the product fix and passes after. | Review product correction | Defect-specific red/green evidence preserves the intended assertion. |
| TI-05 | Replace an exact three-item assertion with a non-null assertion to clear a failure. | Reject weakened assertion | Incorrect counts would now pass. |
| TI-06 | Regenerate every golden snapshot after mismatches without citing intended changes or reviewing the material diff. | Reject rubber-stamped snapshot | Observed output does not establish correctness. |
| TI-07 | Skip an intermittently failing case without a diagnosis, approved scope, or revisit condition. | Reject unexplained skip | A green suite would conceal lost verification. |
| TI-08 | Replace the real persistence component in an integration check with a mock returning the expected value. | Reject coverage-reducing mock | The test no longer exercises the behavior it claims to verify. |
| TI-09 | Dependency restore fails before tests execute. Retry only after isolating the environment issue and retain both outcomes. | Diagnose without changing the oracle | Restore failure is not a failing assertion, even if a later run passes. |
| TI-10 | The old runtime cannot be replayed. Run a property test derived from the accepted ordering rule, with an independently specified counterexample and a stated scope limit. | Review independent alternative | The property can demonstrate the intended behavior without claiming a historical replay. |

This procedure provides a review standard. It does not add automated diff enforcement (FND-165) or the broader requirements-to-test traceability work (FND-171).

For new or materially changed **critical** tests, carry FND-171 oracle provenance in the existing Test Aspects review or linked FND-166 evidence. Name the exact test and intended contract, then derive the expected result from a versioned requirement, accepted ADR or invariant, authoritative specification, worked example, mathematical property, or independently implemented reference. Pin the source to a content digest or immutable revision, with a precise locator. A reviewer must check independence and the derivation, not just the presence of fields. Use the [registered provenance inventory](../../scripts/assurance/regression-evidence/independent-oracles.json) for covered contracts and report the limits of what was inspected.

Do not use the output under test, a golden regenerated from that output, or the same underlying calculation on both sides as independent correctness evidence. Keep genuine characterization and compatibility tests and label their scope. Surface ambiguous or conflicting requirements before changing the behavior the test asserts. For analytical or boundary contracts, seek positive, negative and boundary cases that catch a representative semantic mistake. Do not create a defect receipt solely for an illustrative seeded error.

## 2.4. Mandatory constraint-driven architectural design

**Make correct implementations the easiest implementations, and make invalid states and prohibited operations structurally impossible wherever practical.** This standing rule applies to new and materially changed software in ArchLucid and SecureNow, regardless of whether it is developed by Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex, delegated agents or humans. Constraints follow actual requirements and accepted architectural decisions, not arbitrary restrictions on a particular developer or model.

### Design within explicit constraints

- Before changing a design or shared contract, identify intended behavior, applicable invariant IDs, valid and invalid states, trust boundaries, legitimate extension requirements and affected consumers. Consult the [architecture invariant catalog](../library/ARCHITECTURE_INVARIANTS.md), relevant accepted ADRs and existing canonical types, ports and enforcement. Preserve compatibility and conduct the required shared-component impact analysis; do not introduce a duplicate abstraction.
- Prefer compile-time enforcement and validated domain types over ambiguous primitives when a value carries meaningful domain semantics, identity, units, authorization scope or lifecycle constraints. Reuse canonical types for tenant/workspace/project identifiers, evidence, policies and other established domain concepts. Prefer closed sets or explicit tagged variants for truly closed states; represent unknown, unavailable and incomplete outcomes explicitly rather than silently mapping them to valid defaults. Preserve documented wire/storage contracts at adapters; validate external/persisted values at the trust boundary, including enum membership, nulls, defaults, ranges and deserialization bypasses. Follow the existing [enum boundary audit](../architecture/ENUM_BOUNDARY_AUDIT.md) and validated parser policy. A type alone is not proof that a value is authorized or valid.
- Prefer cohesive, narrowly scoped interfaces and least-authority capabilities. Expose only operations required by legitimate consumers; keep implementation details private/internal where compatible, separate read and mutation capabilities when justified, maintain dependency direction and composition-root ownership. Avoid broad service locators, untyped property bags, generic escape hatches, ambient tenant inference, raw policy bypasses and unconstrained public setters as substitutes for explicit contracts.
- Prefer immutable values, validated construction, explicit state transitions and controlled mutation where these prevent real invalid states or concurrency errors. Use constructors/factories and aggregates to enforce local invariants, with persistence constraints, transactions and runtime guards for constraints that span processes, external state or time. Check default value-type construction, reflection/serialization, database writes, concurrent calls and alternate entry points as applicable; static constraints do not replace runtime authorization, tenant isolation, input validation or durable concurrency control.
- Introduce abstraction and extensibility only for a demonstrated requirement, supported variation or existing architectural seam. Favor a small stable contract over speculative plugin frameworks, configuration switches, generic base classes or proliferating one-method interfaces. Use explicit, bounded extension points with validated inputs/outputs and clear ownership; do not provide an override path that can disable mandatory security, governance or integrity controls. Preserve legitimate flexibility and avoid unnecessary type wrappers, ceremony or interface fragmentation. A constraint must improve correctness, comprehensibility or maintainability in its actual context.

### Verify and evolve constraints

- Apply the [root-cause remediation procedure](#22-mandatory-root-cause-and-systemic-architectural-remediation) when a defect exposes an avoidable degree of freedom. Prefer existing type-system constraints, analyzers, architecture tests and canonical validation; add or strengthen machine enforcement when justified and authorized. Evaluate whether a representative prohibited implementation fails to compile, triggers the expected diagnostic, violates a focused architecture test, or is rejected by the correct runtime/persistence guard. Pair negative controls with valid-use cases and independently justified regression expectations; a compile failure for an unrelated reason is not proof of prevention.
- At design review and completion, identify the constraint, requirement/invariant, affected interfaces and consumers, legitimate extension points, possible bypass paths, enforcement mechanism, actual verification and remaining gaps. Keep documentation proportionate to the change. Do not claim compile-time or class-wide enforcement when only procedural guidance or local runtime validation exists.
- For existing implementations, adopt stronger constraints incrementally within authorized scope; do not initiate mass retyping, broad API breaking changes or speculative refactoring merely to satisfy this rule. Obtain authorization for material scope expansion and incompatible contract changes, plan migration and rollback where appropriate, and track confirmed residual systemic deficiencies in the existing backlog. Retain required test integrity, branch/merge authority and risk-adjusted verification budgets. No comprehensive master CI, gate bypass, customer mutation or new executable enforcement is authorized merely by this rule.

## 2.5. Mandatory incremental architectural conformance and ratcheting

**Retire architectural debt incrementally without allowing it to regenerate.** This standing rule applies to ArchLucid and SecureNow engineering work by Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex, delegated agents and human contributors. It governs adoption and strengthening of enforceable architectural invariants when existing violations cannot safely be corrected in a single change. It supplements the architecture invariant catalog, constraint-driven design, root-cause remediation, shared-component impact analysis and parallel-agent coordination procedures.

### Establish a reviewed baseline

- Identify the governing invariant or accepted contract, its enforcement mechanism, the relevant source revision and the actual inspected scope (projects, languages, generated sources, configuration, runtime/persistence paths and exclusions as applicable). Reuse existing analyzers, architecture tests, CI guards and approved exception mechanisms, including the [enum boundary enforcement approach](../architecture/ENUM_BOUNDARY_AUDIT.md) and FND-098 where relevant. Do not create a competing generic baseline service or duplicate an established guard.
- Inventory existing violations from actual analyzer/test/inspection evidence. Distinguish confirmed violations, legitimate authorized exceptions, suspected violations, uninspected scope and missing enforcement. A historical violation is not automatically an approved exception or proof of acceptable risk. Do not infer zero debt from absent, incomplete or stale diagnostics.
- Use individually identifiable baseline records, not merely an aggregate count: invariant/diagnostic ID, affected project and source location, stable semantic identity or appropriately scoped fingerprint, pinned evidence revision, risk/severity, rationale, linked backlog item or accountable owner, and retirement/review criteria. Reuse existing schemas when available. Reconcile legitimate moves and renames with evidence; never transfer an exception to a different violating implementation or silently approve changed fingerprints.
- Obtain appropriate review and authorization for the initial baseline and any legitimate exception. Do not automatically snapshot a failing build as an approved baseline. Do not grandfather non-exemptable mandatory controls, confirmed material security exposures or release blockers merely because they predate the ratchet. Escalate these according to existing security, release and governance policy; a baseline is not a risk waiver.

### Prevent new violations and retire existing ones

- Where executable enforcement exists or is authorized for implementation, require the comparison against a trusted reviewed baseline from the integration target. Reject new violations, material worsening of an existing violation, unauthorized exception additions/expansion, weaker enforcement and reintroduction of a retired violation. Check identity and affected scope, not just total counts: fixing two historical violations does not permit two different violations to be added.
- Prefer narrow changed-scope enforcement as a transitional step when whole-scope enforcement is not yet feasible. Explicitly report untouched debt and coverage gaps; do not describe a changed-file guard as complete solution-wide prevention. Expand enforcement toward the full applicable scope once the inventory, guard reliability and release evidence support it. Never silently downgrade an existing stronger gate.
- Prioritize historical violations by consequence, exposure, recurrence, shared-component blast radius, dependency order and remediation risk. Preserve immediate and critical release obligations. Plan small, independently verifiable slices; address one or a handful at a time when that reduces destabilization. Coordinate ownership of shared corrections and dependent changes; opportunistic corrections must remain within authorized scope and not compromise an unrelated task.
- After each verified correction, remove the exact retired baseline/exception entry through the existing review process and reconcile the backlog. Where a shared/root-cause correction safely eliminates several violations, verify the affected consumers rather than requiring artificial one-at-a-time work. Do not automatically increase tolerance, rebaseline away failures, disable a detector, weaken assertions or retain stale exemptions to get green CI.
- Any unavoidable exception or policy change requires explicit appropriate authorization, bounded scope, precise rationale, accountable ownership, compensating verification where warranted and expiry or a documented review trigger. Retain the decision and evidence. Emergency exceptions follow existing escalation and gate authority; this rule does not itself authorize bypasses.

### Verify progression and report evidence

- Use focused negative controls demonstrating a genuinely new violation is rejected, an identity substitution with the same total count is rejected, an unauthorized baseline expansion is rejected, and a retired violation cannot recur. Include compliant/unchanged historical cases, legitimate reviewed moves, stale/incomplete evidence and scope changes. Reuse existing gate tests; validate guard changes independently where warranted. If enforcement is not yet executable, state that explicitly and track the missing enforcement through existing backlog procedures.
- Report inspected revision and scope, baseline and remaining violation identities or linked evidence, newly discovered versus newly introduced violations, verified retirements, newly authorized exceptions, guard outcome and unverified work. Distinguish historical inventory changes from actual defect introduction or remediation. Do not count a missing diagnostic, skipped job, failed analyzer execution, timeout or uninspected component as a verified retirement. Preserve the integrity of the previous baseline and compare equivalent evidence before claiming improvement.
- Reconcile parallel changes with current upstream and the combined integration revision before claiming conformance. Apply the existing risk-adjusted verification and release policy; no comprehensive master build, new workflow dispatch, automated merge or unauthorized gate change is implied by this rule. Documentation of a ratchet does not establish that the repository has executable ratchets for every invariant.

## 3. Risk-adjusted verification and cost

Use the existing [test execution model](../library/TEST_EXECUTION_MODEL.md), [build guidance](BUILD.md) and [independent model review practice](../quality/INDEPENDENT_MODEL_CODE_REVIEW.md). The following is a minimum planning guide, not a substitute for applicable required checks:

| Change | Verification plan |
| --- | --- |
| Documentation/instructions only | Inspect scope, references, consistency, syntax/frontmatter and diff; no unnecessary product build. |
| Isolated implementation | Scoped compile/typecheck and targeted behavioral regression. |
| Shared implementation | Affected direct/transitive consumer and contract regression, appropriate scoped Release compile/typecheck. |
| Security or tenant boundary | Relevant negative controls and independent review of material changes, plus the affected tests. |
| API, persistence or serialization contracts | Relevant integration, generated-client and backward-compatibility verification; use real dependencies where required. |
| Cross-cutting architecture or release candidate | Expanded affected verification, independent review of material risks and authorized release gates. |

Use existing scripts and infrastructure. Respect resource budgets, cancellation and time limits without converting incomplete verification into a pass. No comprehensive master CI by default; comprehensive verification belongs on the owner-designated release branch under the current owner-approved policy. Recheck that policy rather than assume a historical release number is still current. Never dispatch a comprehensive build, bypass a required gate, or merge solely to reduce expense without applicable authorization.

## 4. Repository and operational authority

- Check current target branch, revision, pending PRs and affected files before writing; avoid duplicated or conflicting work. Do not overwrite unrelated edits or silently expand scope.
- Follow the active branch-naming, working-tree, commit/push, review and merge policies. Use a feature branch and PR for ordinary changes; do not merge or enable automatic merge without the owner's authorization. Preserve owner-designated release restrictions.
- No production deployment, billing change, secret disclosure, customer-environment mutation or external notification is authorized by these instructions.
- Check the [active technical backlog](../ACTIVE_TECHNICAL_BACKLOG.md) and existing PRs before creating requirements; extend existing work where feasible. Record confirmed backlog changes and distinguish proposed entries, open PRs, merged entries and verified implementation.
- For completed ArchLucid/SecureNow architecture examination exchanges, follow the maintenance contract in the [architecture Q&A](../architecture/QUESTIONS_AND_ANSWERS.md), recording the answer, assessment, refinements and backlog references. Do not invent transcripts or completed work.

## 5. Compact completion checklist

For substantive shared-impact work, the completion summary or PR should identify the requested outcome; target revision and changed paths; applicable invariant IDs; affected consumers/contracts and remaining unknowns; regression and verification evidence; unverified work and blockers; and actual branch/PR status. Keep this proportional to the work. For read-only and documentation-only tasks, report the relevant static checks and limitations rather than claiming product test results.
